Status tags: **DECIDED** = the builder's decision; **PROPOSED** = derived by analysis, awaiting the builder's confirmation; **OPEN** = unresolved. The thrust-versus-drag analysis behind every PROPOSED number is in section 11; the scripts that produce it are in the Simulation & Analysis folder. This doc states current values only; history of changes is in the Build Log.

## The concept

A foiling AC40-style monohull for a 10-12 kt true-wind window, sailing about 10 kt upwind, on the foil from no more than 8.75 kt of wind. The cant-arm reach is capped at 250 mm. Righting moment is the leeward foil's total lift (weight plus the rudder-downforce reaction it must also carry) acting through its distance from the centreline (the arm pivot's 114.6 mm offset plus the 250 mm reach, 364.6 mm), so it is set by weight, rudder trim, pivot position and reach together, not weight alone: the boat is about 2.8 kg, 2.4 times a length-cubed scaling of the prior-build reference in the Weight Budget. The weight is put where it is free or useful. The wet part of each arm and the T-foil are solid steel (Foilarm_v5: the whole foil; Foilarm_v3: the forward 75% of its chord) (a low, load-carrying ballast that adds no drag), the steel arm section is small and thin to cut drag, and a carbon hollow upper arm carries the bending. About 0.13 kg of lead sits in the hull between the pivot bulkheads (0.84 kg until 28 Oct 2026; the Foilarm_v5 arms, the gimbal rev E mechanism with its rake servos and the real Hull_v2 skin took the difference). The sail is sized from the wind needed to take off, then depowered by the traveller and mainsheet, because the righting moment, not the sail, sets the maximum heeling force.

## Rejected (with reason)

- Sliding ballast (hull width).
- Ballast bulbs at the arm tips (extra wetted area and drag).
- Filling the hollow arm with ballast (the cavity of a 38 mm, 6-ply arm is only 47 mm2, about 110-250 g per arm depending on material).
- A larger foil (see above).
- A wide, low rig (at 2.0 kg and 300 mm reach it was slightly slower than the soft rig, 9.0 against 9.1 kt at 10 kt of wind: the sail efficiency lost cancels the lower centre of effort).
- Carbon struts as thin as 12% (the structural margin falls with thickness squared; the twist and bending checks settle on about 17%).
- Annealed 316L for the steel arm (it needs about 35 mm chord and 275 g of steel for a 6% drag saving, against about 19% in 17-4PH).
- A 6-ply flap shell as the twist fix (about the same added mass as the spring fix and no new machining, but the builder preferred springs; see the decision log).
- The scarf steel-to-carbon joint (the steel thinning to an edge under a skin that keeps the 22 mm wet profile; 1.7 g lighter per arm and no drag cost, but a 3D-machined steel taper with a thin stainless edge): the builder chose straight steel, 29 Oct 2026.
