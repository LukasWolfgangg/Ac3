# Design & Specs - 9. Weight Budget, 10. Design Risks / Open Questions

Source: Google Doc "Design & Specs" (30 Oct 2026). Status tags as in 00-concept-and-decision-log.md.

## 9. Weight Budget

See the Weight Budget spreadsheet. Nominal all-up weight is 2800 g against a 2800 g target (low about 2452 g, high about 3388 g for the estimates; the ballast, about 128 g, is a design value trimmed at the final weighing to hit the target; at that size it covers an overrun of only about 5% on everything else, and the high estimate is about 588 g over the target; the all-up CG is x 482 mm from the transom, section 8). Design numbers at 2.80 kg: **physical righting moment 15.12 Nm** (on the 22 kt top-reach structural basis; includes the rudder-downforce reaction; lever 364.6 mm with the pivot at 84% of the Hull_v2 half-beam), **design cant torque 13.48 Nm**, **design heeling force 49.7 N** (19.7 Nm design heeling moment at the 0.395 m centre of effort), sail 0.44 m2, rudder downforce 14.01 N (the structural basis, the 22 kt top reach; the pitch balance gives about 0.7-5.6 N up to 14.5 kt, sections 8 and 11), takeoff speed 5.65 kt at CL 0.55 with the pitch-balance rudder load (6.44 kt on the old 8.74 N rule).

## 10. Design Risks / Open Questions

- **Dynamic/maneuvering cant torque still unmodeled - HIGHEST remaining structural-risk priority:** the static design torque is already about 3.8 times the old design's. The upset is now a design case (DECIDED 28 Oct 2026, section 0): the steady top-reach strut side force reversed adds 7.7 Nm (21.2 Nm in total), checked as a survival case at 1.5x on yield for metal and 2x on failure for carbon. Every gimbal part passes (tongue 2.3x, cross shaft 2.5x, carbon arm root with its doubler 2.95x) except a single worm-wheel tooth carrying the whole load (1.2x), which waits on HPC's static rating (section 8; Mechanisms - Foil Arm section 2d).
- **Structural basis raised to the top reach (DECIDED 29 Oct 2026, the builder's decision):** the design loads now come from the 22.15 kt reach in 12 kt of wind (TWA 105 deg): foil 41.48 N, design cant torque 13.48 Nm, rudder downforce 14.01 N (section 3). Gimbal rev E3 fixes the three parts that fell under their bars (two 3 mm sector pins, 5 mm rake-fork cheeks, and a 0/90 root doubler on the carbon arm) and retunes the cant-drive spur pair to 2.5:1. Raising the basis showed three more problems: the rake servo has a PROPOSED fix that still needs a bench test, the rudder at speed is not fixed yet (next two items); the steel-to-carbon joint under the strut side force now passes on paper with the builder's straight-steel layout and a UD sleeve (below; coupon test open). Above 12 kt of wind, outside the window, the reach load keeps rising (43.4 N at 13 kt, 45.3 N at 14 kt), still inside the x1.3 but using most of it.
- **Rake servo at speed (PROPOSED, 29 Oct 2026):** the AGF RC A66BHLW on the 5:1 linkage holds the 4.56 Nm design rake moment at 3.23x (7.4 V) and the steady top-reach hold (3.51 Nm, 7.2 kg.cm at the servo) at 24% of stall, inside the ASSUMED one-third duty rule; its figures are retailer listings with no current data, so bench-test one before buying the second (section 8).
- **Rudder at speed (PROPOSED throw limit, 29 Oct 2026; rudder_limit.py, Mechanisms - Rudder section 2c):** the T-foil carries its 14 N easily; the blade and post are bent by the blade's own side force. Measured on Hull_v2 the stern flies 84 mm clear at the design ride, so a 220 mm blade put the T-foil at the main foil's depth (136 mm wetted); the 240 mm blade (DECIDED 4 Nov 2026) puts it 155.8 mm under water. A 34 mm, 17%, 4-ply blade thin all the way reaches its 4x bar at 0.66 deg at 22 kt (220 mm: limit = 324 / V^2 degrees, V in kt, while foiling, 79% of that hull-borne, capped at the 25 deg mechanical throw below about 3.5 kt; for the chosen blade the hull-borne limit is 95% of the foiling limit at 6.5 kt, rudder_envelope.py section 8). With the root thickened in the 84 mm that is dry while foiling (thin from 15 mm above the design waterline down, about 40 mm x 25% at the hull) and the thin section tapering from 34 mm to 20 mm at the T-foil, the limit is 1.22 deg at 22 kt (598 / V^2; the 220 mm uniform blade was 1.21 deg, 594 / V^2) and the stock needs 5.7 mm 17-4PH at 3x on yield (6 mm drawn: 3.4x; rudder_limit.py last block, rudder_sweep.py); bearing loads at that limit 69 N upper and 108 N lower (hull-bottom moment 6.17 Nm, bearings 89.7 mm apart). That costs no control: a steady 1 g turn needs 0.08 deg at 22 kt and 0.37 deg at 10 kt, because the main strut, 35 mm ahead of the CG, carries almost all the side force. It needs a boat-speed input on the flight controller (a GPS, for example), or a fixed foiling limit at the 22 kt value. Rake moment about the trunnion (246 mm above the T-foil with the 240 mm blade): 1.57 Nm with x1.3 at 22 kt and 5 deg of rake (1.55 Nm for the 220 mm blade). Since rev B the rake rod acts 27 mm above the trunnion, so that is 58.1 N with x1.3 at the AGF A50BHLW rake servo (57.4 N for the 220 mm blade); the steady 44.2 N and 3.6 kg.cm (20% of its stall torque) were for the 220 mm blade and rise by about the same 1.3%, not re-derived. Rake concept A DECIDED (29 Oct 2026, the builder) and drawn in Hull_v2 (Rudder_A, rev B in version 21, tripod socketed in version 22): the whole rudder rakes +-5.5 deg about a trunnion at the hull bottom in a wet well, its carbon cassette is guided by a three-strut tripod at the stern, and both servos sit below deck, the A50BHLW for rake and a Savox SW-0250MG for steering (Mechanisms - Rudder section 2d).
- **Main foil section (E226): what the 3D check leaves open.** The foil.tools AeroBuildup check (27 Sep 2026, no free-surface effect) puts the span efficiency at 0.77 against the 0.85 the VPP assumes, so induced drag is about 10% above the model; wing CLmax is 0.93, 1.67x the takeoff lift coefficient, with a root-first stall. In turbulent water (Ncrit 5) E817 loses less, so E226's advantage over it shrinks to about 1.2 kt. The steel foil bends at about 36 MPa at the top-reach design load (28x on yield). Confirm on the water if wanted: no tow test is planned (dropped 20 Oct 2026, no boat or tow stream), so this check waits for speed runs on the first boat.
- **Real drag is still the dominant unknown.** On paper the boat does 15.3 kt upwind at 10 kt of wind (about 53% over the 10 kt target) and 16.3 / 17.3 kt at 11 / 12 kt of wind (pitch-balance rudder load, section 11). Drag 30% high still gives 13.5 kt at 10 kt of wind, so the target survives the drag uncertainty on paper. The model's drag is uncertain by about +-30% (13.5-17.5 kt upwind at 10 kt of wind) and the centre-of-effort height by +-20% (14.5-15.9 kt), and the strut-drag calibration (next item) is weaker than that range suggests. **Recommendation: measure real drag and centre-of-effort height with on-the-water speed runs on the first hull (GPS speed against wind; Build Task Tracker). A tow test was dropped on 20 Oct 2026: no boat or tow stream is available, and a foil-only test would not cover the hull drag before takeoff. A bad drag number is more likely to cost part of a healthy margin than to decide whether the boat reaches 10 kt, but it can decide whether the 8.75 kt takeoff target holds (break-even in section 11).** Pushing the all-up weight up for more margin is not needed (section 11).
- **Strut-drag calibration constant is anchored to a retired figure.** The strut/arm/blade drag model (section 11) uses a flat calibration constant CAL = 18.4/25.7 ~ 0.716 tying the flat-plate friction model to "the project's published 18.4 N figure at 25 kt for the old 36 mm arm" - but that 25 kt case, and the 18.4 N figure with it, were retired with the old speed cases (5/14/20/25 kt). The arm and blade drag numbers used throughout section 11 (and this doc) are calibrated against a data point the project no longer asserts is real. This doesn't necessarily make the drag numbers wrong - the calibration may still be a reasonable scaling - but it means the +-30% drag uncertainty above is probably an underestimate of how little is actually anchored here, and it should be near the top of the list for the on-water speed-run recommendation above.
- **Steel-to-carbon joint:** passes the top-reach basis on paper since 29 Oct 2026 (straight steel at full section to 135 mm in a tapered UD carbon sleeve, Foilarm_v5 version 10; section 8); the coupon test is OPEN. The joint (135 mm) sits almost exactly at the design waterline, so it is wet much of the time, and its bond strength, peel, fatigue and galvanic behaviour are untested. Under the 22 kt top reach's strut side force (38.0 N, 3.3 Nm x 1.3 at the joint) the glue line is at 3.6x on an assumed 15 MPa wet strength, the steel at 7.1x and the carbon at 5.9x or more; it works only with UD carbon in the sleeve (woven: glue 1.3x). The cost is 11.3 g per arm and 0.1 kt upwind. Coupon before making the pair: a 60 mm length of the real strut and sleeve (steel bar, glass ply, UD + +-45, the M1.6 screw), a week in salt water, then loaded as a cantilever at the steel's end to at least 10 Nm (3x the 3.33 Nm design moment) without bond failure; a second coupon taken to failure gives the real margin and the UD strength.
- **Foilarm_v4 pivot, roll capacity:** the 1.2 mm tang yields at 0.99 Nm of roll moment (a 27 mm shift of the lift's centre of pressure) and the slot walls at 1.3 to 1.8 Nm; losing lift over the outer quarter of one half-span gives 1.3x on yield against the 3x bar. Section 8 (Foilarm_v4 local strength check) has the numbers and the open decision. Foilarm_v3 does not have this weak point. Modelled fix: variant D (section 8) reaches 3.3 to 3.8x at 1000 MPa yield but only 2.4 to 2.8x at 750 MPa. DECIDED (Lukas, 20 Oct 2026): build Foilarm_v3 first; Foilarm_v4 is shelved until the first hull's speed runs and flight-controller tuning show whether the all-moving foil is needed. Reason: v4 needs about 0.2 degree of foil-angle precision from a 4.2 mm control arm and a 1.2 mm pin, and play or wire stretch of a tenth of a millimetre costs about half the lift. If v4 resumes: proof-load a variant D first article to 2.3 Nm (method: Tests folder, Foilarm_v4 Tang Bench Test) and consider moving the pivot up to the carbon-arm joint.
- **Steel underwater unit:** manufacturing route, cost, the 222 g fixed-element estimate and the CAD mass (277 g per unit), and the surface finish on the drag-critical wet surfaces are unproven. 17-4PH resists chloride pitting less well than 316; rinse after salt water.
- **Flap spring mounts:** a CAD check that the three new spring anchor points fit in the steel fixed element, and the design of those spring-mount features, are still open (Foil Arm Mechanisms, open items). Now also: the springs are to form the hinge line with no pins (Lukas, 20 Oct 2026), the centre spring station clashes with the new flap tongue, and the tongue reverses the actuation sense (springs hold the flap up, cable pulls it down; fails toward less lift), which needs confirming (Mechanisms - Foil Arm section 2a).
- **Hull drag before takeoff** uses an assumed resistance table, so the 6.25 kt takeoff wind is not solid (7.5 kt on the 8.74 N rudder rule, and 7.75 kt before the longer Hull_v2 waterline went into the same assumed table, 0.98 against 0.90 m; takeoff is not righting-moment-capped). The sail polar (CL up to 1.3, drag polar 0.06 + CL^2/(pi.AR.0.8)) is a generic soft-rig assumption.
- **Rudder T-foil:** with the pitch-balance load it works at a lift coefficient of 0.06 or less at every speed, so the old takeoff stall concern is gone, and its 14 N structural load is easy. The open rudder items are the steering load and the rake moment at speed (item above). Details in Rudder Mechanism sections 2a, 2b and 6.
- **Hull internal structure and pivot root mounts:** rough scaling only, no structural design yet; design them for the 13.48 Nm design cant torque plus the 4.75 Nm carried across the hull from the pivot offset (section 3).
- **Rig:** the mast (1.14 m luff), boom and jib servo are not designed for the 0.44 m2 sail.
- **Power tree (PROPOSED 28 Oct 2026):** the 6 V cant gearmotors run from the 2S battery through PWM-limited brushed-DC drivers (TB9051FTG class, one per arm), and the HV rake servos run on 2S directly; the rudder's AGF A50BHLW rake servo runs on 2S too, and only its Savox SW-0250MG steering servo (4.8-6 V) needs a 6 V BEC. Still to check: the battery and wiring current budget, with about 2.1 A per cant motor at the design torque (2.9 A at stall; gimbal_revE_calcs.py); the A66BHLW rake servos list no current figures, so their holding current comes from the bench test.
