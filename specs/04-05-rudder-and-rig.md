## 4. Rudder & Steering

Rudder downforce 14.01 N (DECIDED 29 Oct 2026 as the structural basis): the pitch-balance load on the 22 kt top reach of the design window (section 3), 0.510 x the all-up weight in opt.py, so it follows the Weight Budget. For performance the VPP uses the pitch-balance load speed by speed: about 0.7 N at 6.5 kt, 2.6 N at 10 kt and 5.7 N at 14.5 kt (section 11, re-run 30 Oct 2026 at the rudder rev B CG). Both rest on the CG layout of section 8, so the ballast placement moves them, and the righting moment with them. The downforce is axial in the blade: it loads the T-foil (10x in solid carbon at 14 N x 1.3) and the post's axial bearing, but it does not bend the blade or the post. They are bent by the blade's own side force, which at 22 kt is about 18-22 N per degree of blade incidence, so a 220 mm, 4-ply blade reaches its 4x bar at 0.66 deg at 22 kt, the PROPOSED throw limit (section 10; Mechanisms - Rudder section 2c). T-foil S9033 (120 x 30 mm) is kept; with the pitch-balance load it works at a section lift coefficient of 0.06 or less at every speed from takeoff to the top reach (about 0.02 at takeoff), far from stall. Blade: E864-family, 220 mm below the hull (PROPOSED), 40 mm x 25% at the hull tapering to 34 mm x 17% from 69 mm down, 4-ply carbon, about 21 g; T-foil solid carbon, 7.2 g (CAD, Hull_v2 Rudder_A). The thin section cuts blade drag from 1.78 N to 0.93 N at 10 kt (136 mm wetted at the design ride). Real checks in the Rudder Mechanism doc. **Flagged 27 Sep 2026:** at the new 135 mm design ride the 150 mm blade (and the T-foil it carries) will not be fully submerged; it likely needs to lengthen toward about 230-260 mm. Measured on Hull_v2 (29 Oct 2026): 220 mm below the hull puts the T-foil at the main foil's depth (PROPOSED, section 10).

## 5. Rig & Sails

- **Sail-area budget: 0.44 m2 (DECIDED 27 Sep 2026).** The old rule (design torque / centre-of-effort height = design heeling force, then area in proportion) sized the sail from heel torque and is retired; the sail is sized by thrust versus drag (section 11). With the pivot at 84% of the Hull_v2 half-beam the righting moment does not cap the boat at the bottom of the window, so the sail is the limit there: 0.44 m2 gets onto the foil from 6.25 kt of wind (0.40 m2: 6.5 kt), and against 0.40 m2 it is 0.2 kt faster upwind at 10 kt of wind and level at 11 and 12 kt (section 11, with the pitch-balance rudder load). From about 8.75 kt of wind the righting moment, not the sail, sets the driving force, so the sail is depowered with the traveller and mainsheet.
- **Heeling limits.** Centre-of-effort height is taken as 0.395 m (0.25 m at 0.176 m2, scaled by sqrt(area): an ASSUMPTION, measure it once the rig is drawn). **Physical limit 15.12 Nm / 0.395 m = 38.3 N on the structural basis (the 22 kt top reach; 26-31 N up to 14.5 kt of boat speed, section 11); design value with the 1.30 margin 49.7 N** (a 19.7 Nm design heeling moment, whatever the sail area). (The righting moment includes the rudder-downforce reaction and acts through the 364.6 mm from the centreline to the foil, with the pivot at 84% of the Hull_v2 half-beam; section 3.) The rig's centre of effort is estimated at about 533 mm from the transom (main about 410 mm, jib about 733 mm, weighted by area), within about 10 mm of the main strut at 526 mm, so the strut, not the rudder, carries the sail's side force (ASSUMPTION until the rig is drawn).
- **Mainsail sizing: shape CONFIRMED, area DECIDED.** Real AC75-derived aspect ratio (luff:foot) of 2.4:1 with a square-top head. Split with the real AC75 main:jib ratio (61.7% / 38.3%): **mainsail ~ 0.272 m2** (luff ~ 1.14 m, foot ~ 0.48 m at AR 2.4), **jib ~ 0.168 m2** (dimensions/AR still open - see section 8). The mast therefore stands about 1.15 m above the deck (1.14 m of luff plus the foot clearance, assuming a deck-sweeping foot like the AC40), plus whatever runs below deck to the step: taller than the hull is long. For comparison the AC40's mast is 17.92 m on 11.80 m LOA (1.52x LOA) and its main alone would be about 0.45 m2 at this length; this rig is lower and smaller than a scaled AC40 because the area comes from the thrust-versus-drag balance and the shape from AR 2.4, not from AC40 proportions.
- **Standing rigging: CONFIRMED - real, complete configuration.** Masthead sloop, no spreaders, no backstay. 1 forestay (masthead to bow) + 2 shrouds (masthead to deck chainplates, swept slightly aft). Aft-swept shrouds balance the forestay's forward pull directly, avoiding the extra wire, weight and attachment point of a backstay. No spreaders: they support a long mast under high compression, which is unnecessary at this height and load (recheck shroud tension against the **49.7 N** design heeling force (19.7 Nm design heeling moment) once the beam is known). Material: thin Dyneema for forestay and both shrouds (strength-to-weight, low stretch). Required component: a mast rotation collar/fitting, because the mast rotates and the shrouds cannot attach directly to the tube; it holds a fixed rotational reference while the tube spins inside it. A directly comparable IOM-class RC sailboat reference confirms this approach (thin coated line, crimps, small sliding bowsie adjusters).
- Traveller, mainsheet and mast rotation as in the Rig & Sail Mechanism doc (vang removed). The traveller and mainsheet are the main depowering tools above 8.75 kt of wind.
