# Design & Specs - 3. Foils & Ride-Height Control

Source: Google Doc "Design & Specs" (30 Oct 2026). Status tags as in 00-concept-and-decision-log.md.

- **Foil: E226, 40 mm chord, 300 mm span, 0.012 m2 area (DECIDED 27 Sep 2026; the section was E817).** Eppler E226, 10.2% thick, 0.8% camber. T-shaped, flat planform, elliptical tip caps. Chord taper was evaluated in several configurations and rejected (low-Reynolds-number tip penalties at this scale). E817 was dropped because its low-drag range sits around section CL 0.65-0.8, while this foil flies at 0.1-0.25 once up; E226 roughly halves the foil's profile drag there, cuts its pitching moment to about a third and narrows the angle-of-attack range (section 10). The 40 x 300 mm planform was re-checked against 34-46 mm chords and 260-350 mm spans and kept.
- **Steel underwater unit (DECIDED concept).** Per side, one solid 17-4PH stainless piece (H1025 heat treatment, yield about 1000 MPa, typical value, not certified) made of the foil's fixed element (the forward 75% of the E226 chord over the full 300 mm span, about 222 g) and the lower arm section (below), about 51 g with the old spigot; Foilarm_v5 (29 Oct 2026) runs the full 22 mm x 18% section straight to 135 mm, about 59 g, and its whole steel unit with the all-steel foil is 305.8 g (CAD). The CAD model (Foilarm_v3) gives 277 g for the whole unit, so the pair is about 0.55 kg of low, load-carrying ballast. Making it as one machined or metal-3D-printed piece is PROPOSED; the builder expects to CNC-machine it (29 Oct 2026), so the steel stays straight, with no bends.
- **Lower arm section (steel, wet).** E864-family outline thinned to 18% t/c, 22 mm chord (4.0 mm thick), straight and vertical from the foil line to 135 mm (**DECIDED 27 Sep 2026**, the builder's choice: keep the wet arm straight for ease of build, and fly the design ride about 112 mm higher instead of extending the steel to the old 280 mm joint). CAD (Foilarm_v3) confirms the wetted length at the design ride is now the full 135 mm straight run (Foilarm_v3: the carbon starts dry at 135 mm; Foilarm_v5: the carbon joint sleeve over its top 40 mm, 95-135 mm, is wet, and the carbon is dry from 135 mm to the pivot). At 10 kt its drag is **0.79 N** per arm (0.82 N in Foilarm_v5, whose joint sleeve thickens the top 40 mm; joint_straight.py). Strength margin about 469x on yield at the joint for the lift-offset moment (below) - the joint now sits on the still-vertical run, so it barely bends; the section's size is no longer set by bending there (section 8).
- **Upper arm (carbon, dry).** E864-family outline, 40 mm chord, 20% t/c, hollow shell, 6 plies of 200 g/m2 carbon (recommended: 1 x 0/90 + 5 x bias-cut +-45). In Foilarm_v5 (CAD version 10, 29 Oct 2026) it is three zones in one moulding: the joint sleeve outside the straight steel (95-135 mm, wet; UD carbon, 0.1 to 1.5 mm a side), a hollow loft from the 30.8 x 7.0 mm section at 135 mm into the 40 mm profile about 40 mm along the bend, then the hollow 40 mm/20% sweep to the pivot end; mass 44.2 g per arm (CAD) plus the root doubler. In Foilarm_v3 it was three pieces (a 0.93 mm sleeve over a steel spigot at 105-135 mm, a solid loft at 135-175 mm and the sweep; 47 g). It carries the bending, and above 135 mm it is dry and costs no drag; its size is set by structure only: 20% t/c is the thinnest section that cleared the >=4.0x root-margin bar on the old 11.77 Nm basis (4.13x). DECIDED. On the top-reach basis (13.48 Nm) the 6-ply section is at 3.61x at the root, so a 7th ply, 0/90, goes over the root as a doubler from the arm end to 60 mm along the arm, tapering out by 75 mm (PROPOSED 29 Oct 2026): 4.64x, about 2.5 g per arm. The 6-ply sweep clears 4x from about 25 mm (horizontal) out from the pivot. The doubler is on the outside, so the hull-side slot needs about 0.3 mm more clearance there.
- **Joint.** At 135 mm (the top of the straight vertical run), the builder's decision 27 Sep 2026; layout the builder's decision 29 Oct 2026 (joint_straight.py). The steel strut runs straight at its full 22 mm x 18% section to 135 mm - no spigot, no taper, no bends, so the steel unit is one constant profile from the foil up, the simplest part to CNC-machine - and the carbon is a sleeve on its outside that thickens from a 0.1 mm feather edge at 95 mm to 1.5 mm a side at 135 mm, where the steel ends, closing in a short fair wedge behind the steel's trailing edge (30.8 x 7.0 mm, 22.6%, at 135 mm). The sleeve must be unidirectional carbon along the strut (about 1.3 mm a side at 135 mm, plies dropped in steps down to 95 mm and run on about 20 mm past the steel's end) with one +-45 ply outside; epoxy bond with one glass ply between the steel and the carbon as a galvanic barrier, and one M1.6 x 5 countersunk titanium screw from the side at 125 mm to locate the parts and back up the glue - at the design waterline, so the barrier and edge seal matter (section 10). On the 22 kt top-reach side force the glue line is at 3.6x on an assumed 15 MPa wet strength, the steel at 7.1x and the carbon at 5.9x or more (section 8). Bond strength, UD strength and fatigue are untested (section 10); coupon-test before building the pair.
- **Cant-arm structural load.** The arm, joint and actuators react the vertical load the deployed foil generates: weight plus the rudder downforce. The structural basis (DECIDED 29 Oct 2026) is that load on the top reach of the design window, 12 kt of wind at a true wind angle of 105 deg and 22.15 kt of boat speed, where the pitch-balance rudder downforce is 14.01 N and the foil carries 41.48 N (top_reach.py; opt.py's RUD_STRUCT, 0.510 x weight). With 30% more drag the reach is slower but the load 3% higher (42.66 N), inside the x1.3; in 13-14 kt of wind, outside the window, it reaches 43-45 N. Re-run on 30 Oct 2026 at the rudder rev B CG (481.8 mm) with the 136 mm wetted rudder blade, the top reach is 21.86 kt with 13.64 N on the rudder, 41.11 N on the foil and 13.36 Nm of design cant torque, so the basis, kept at the 29 Oct values, still envelopes it (section 8). The sail's steady side force on that reach is 38.0 N (the heel cap binds), and the leeward strut carries it, because the rig's centre of effort is estimated within about 10 mm of the strut station (section 5). **Static arm-load moment = 41.48 N x 0.25 m = 10.37 Nm; design value (x1.30) = 13.48 Nm.** The gimbal rev E3 parts, the cant drive and the carbon arm with its root doubler clear their bars against it (PROPOSED, Mechanisms - Foil Arm sections 1 and 2d); the AGF A66BHLW rake servo meets the design rake moment and the ASSUMED duty rule on its listed figures (bench test open), and the straight-steel joint passes on paper with a coupon test still open (section 8). The pivot bulkheads' bond to the hull has not been checked. **The righting moment on this basis is 15.12 Nm (10.3-12.1 Nm up to 14.5 kt of boat speed, section 11):** the same 41.48 N (the leeward foil's lift also has to cancel the rudder's own downforce, a moment balance about the boat's CG), acting through the foil's full distance from the centreline, 114.6 mm pivot offset + 250 mm reach = 364.6 mm. The cant actuator only sees the 250 mm part; the 114.6 mm part is carried by the hull from the pivot to the centreline (4.75 Nm). The VPP's heel-force cap follows the righting moment speed by speed (sections 5 and 11).
- **Joint moment.** Mj = 13.48 Nm x 1.25 (cant-tilt/side-force factor) x 1/250 (horizontal offset of the joint from the foil, over the reach - about 1 mm, since the joint is on the vertical run) = about **0.05 Nm**. Steel bending stress about 2.1 MPa, margin about 469x on yield - nowhere near the >=3x bar, because there is almost no lever arm left. Carbon root margin 3.61x at 40 mm/20%/6 plies, under the >=4.0x bar, hence the root doubler (4.64x, above). Twist at 0.29 Nm (the E226 foil's pitching moment at 14 kt): **0.09 deg** (steel 0.01 deg, carbon 0.08 deg); on the 22 kt top reach the pitching moment is 0.78 Nm and the twist about 0.24 deg. Twist comes from the foil's own pitching moment, not the cant load. All from steel.py's hybrid() (r250c.py). This is the lift-offset term only: on the top reach the strut's own side force puts 3.3 Nm on the joint (38.0 N x 1.3 over the 135 mm wet length), which the straight-steel joint carries with its UD sleeve (glue line 3.6x, section 8).
- **Flap and T-foil.** Foilarm_v3, the fallback (Foilarm_v5 has no flap and rakes the whole arm; Mechanisms - Foil Arm section 2d). Split fixed/flap construction; the fixed element is steel (above) and the flap is carbon on the E226 trailing 25% (260 mm span). The section is thinner than two 4-ply walls (1.86 mm) aft of the hinge (1.91 mm there), so the flap is a tapered solid laminate, about 4.5 g per side (Weight Budget), not an open shell; non-uniform flap deflection is accepted as a design decision. Hinge moment at the design load, conservative bound: 45.25 mNm (independent of the section; E226 carries less load near the trailing edge, so the real value should be lower). Twist: about 1.7 deg with the original four springs (three 80 mm bays); 0.42 deg with the decided seven springs per side (-80, 0 and +80 mm added, every bay 40 mm; flap_steel_e226.py), for +6 g/side and three new spring-mount features in the not-yet-designed steel fixed element. Flap travel +-7 deg (PROPOSED, see mount pitch below). Foil Arm Mechanisms section 2a has the history; the Weight Budget has the mass lines.
- **Mount pitch: +3.3 deg geometric, flap travel +-7 deg (PROPOSED 27 Sep 2026).** From the 3D check of the whole foil at 36.21 N (foil.tools AeroBuildup): E226 needs +5.0 deg at 6.5 kt down to +0.2 deg at 14.5 kt (4.8 deg spread; E817 needed +4.5 to -2.3 deg, 6.8 deg). A deflected-geometry NeuralFoil run gives about +1.5-2.0 deg of equivalent angle of attack for +5 deg of flap (less at the takeoff Reynolds number) and -2.7 deg for -5 deg, an effectiveness of about 0.3-0.55 rather than the 0.6 assumed earlier. At +3.3 deg the flap has to supply about +1.7 deg at takeoff (about +5.5 deg of flap) and -3.1 deg at 14.5 kt (about -5.7 deg), so +-7 deg of travel leaves about 1-1.5 deg of margin at each end; +-5 deg would not cover the range. The old +2.07 deg pitch was set for the 1.2 kg boat and is superseded. Confirm in the first flight tests: the free surface, ventilation and the real flap effectiveness are not modeled.
- **Cant control (settled, 20 Sep 2026):** the pilot raises and lowers each cant arm. While an arm is down, the flight controller sets its exact cant angle (in degrees), driven by heel (IMU roll).
- **Cant torque: design 13.48 Nm/arm, physical righting moment 15.12 Nm (structural basis).** Both use the same load: the deployed foil's lift on the top reach, weight plus the 14.01 N rudder downforce (41.48 N total). The design cant torque is 1.30x that load x the 250 mm reach (structural, about the pivot); the physical righting moment is that load x 364.6 mm (the 114.6 mm pivot offset plus the reach), unmargined (sailing). Use 13.48 Nm to size the cant actuators, brackets and arm, 15.12 Nm wherever the physical righting moment on the structural basis is meant, and design the root mounts and pivot bulkheads for the cant torque plus the load carried across the hull (41.48 N x 114.6 mm = 4.75 Nm static). **Raised windward arm: a small gain, as long as it is not over-rotated.** The arm raises by swinging outboard and up about its pivot, the same as the AC75 and AC40 (AC75 Class Rule V3.01: cant axis longitudinal, 2050 mm off the centreline and 520 mm above the waterplane; cant angle 10 deg has the wing tucked under the hull, the wing is level at 41.4 deg, racing range 10-90 deg, fully raised above 115 deg, 119.5 deg required). Swinging the other way would drive the foil deeper and under the hull, so it is not an option. Rotating the traced arm (arm_pts.csv, 338 g per side rotating: steel foil element, flap and springs, steel arm, carbon arm, with the joint at 135 mm) about the pivot moves the combined centre of gravity to windward by +0.28 Nm at 30 deg past the down position, +0.31 Nm at 45 deg (the maximum), +0.28 at 60 deg, +0.21 at 70 deg, and back to zero at about 90 deg; beyond that the foil comes back inboard over the deck and it costs righting moment (-0.48 Nm at 120 deg). The AC75's own fully raised position corresponds to about 69 deg on this arm (foil about 140 mm above pivot height, about 21 deg above horizontal from the pivot), so +0.21 Nm. Do not copy the AC75's 119.5 deg literally: its cant angle is measured from the arm hanging straight down, not from the foiling position, so 119.5 deg there corresponds to about 69 deg on this arm. The gain does not depend on where the pivot sits across the boat (the arm rotates about its own pivot), so the pivot's position across the boat (84% of the half-beam, section 8) leaves these numbers unchanged. The righting moments here and in section 11 still exclude this gain (conservative); at +0.21 Nm it is worth about +0.1 kt upwind at 10-11 kt of wind and +0.2 kt at 12 kt (raised_arm_vpp.py).
- **Ride height.** The sonar setpoint is the hull height above the water. Foil depth is the arm drop minus the hull height, and depends on cant angle, roll and pitch, so it must be computed from the sonar reading and the cant angle. Keep at least about 3 chords (120 mm) of water above the shallowest foil tip to avoid ventilation and surface effect. **Flagged 27 Sep 2026:** with the steel-to-carbon joint now at 135 mm, the design ride sits about 112 mm higher than before (135 mm wetted instead of 247 mm), so that 120 mm clearance leaves only about 15 mm of slack - about **5.7 deg** of heel or pitch before a tip ventilates, against a large margin at the old ride height. Confirm the real ride height and ventilation behaviour in flight tests before trusting this. Decided 28 Sep 2026: manage the margin with dynamic ride-height compensation - a heel/pitch feedforward term on the sonar setpoint (Flight Control Electronics Build Plan section 2), raising the target ride height as commanded heel or pitch increases - rather than lowering the fixed ride-height target or changing the joint position now. A ventilation fence and revisiting the 135 mm joint stay as fallback options if flight testing shows the margin still isn't enough. CHECKED (Claude, 20 Oct 2026, panel-method model of the 40 x 300 mm foil with a free-surface image, upper-bound high-speed case, flat plate at 4 degrees): at 135 mm depth the nearby surface costs about 2.4% of lift (2.8% at 5.7 degrees of roll, 3.3% at 8); at 100 mm about 3.6%. The lift penalty is therefore small and is not what limits the ride; the limit is the 15 mm of slack before the shallowest tip is within 3 chords (120 mm) of the surface, which is also a 15 mm drop in ride height from a wave or pitch, not only 5.7 degrees of roll. The 3-chord rule itself is a rule of thumb, and ventilation down the surface-piercing strut is not modelled.
