## Neither twist nor joint strength sizes the steel section any more

The load that twists the foil is its own pitching moment, M = CM x q x S x c (E226 from NeuralFoil at the operating angle of attack; S 0.012 m2, c 0.04 m). Lift is not a twisting load because it acts in the plane of the arm. With E817 (CM about -0.10) twist was the limit on the steel section; E226's CM is about -0.03, so the twist falls to about a fifth, and with the joint at 135 mm the joint bending moment is almost zero (section 3). The 22 mm x 18% section is therefore kept by choice, not set by either check: in the model a thinner section (for example 20 mm x 10%) still passes both and has less drag, but the model has no check for side load on the vertical run, for the arm canted off its design angle, or for impact, so it has not been re-optimized. Results:

| Speed | Section CL | E226 CM | Pitching moment | Arm twist |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6.5 kt (takeoff) | 0.54 | -0.033 | 0.09 Nm | 0.03 deg |
| 10 kt (upwind) | 0.23 | -0.035 | 0.22 Nm | 0.07 deg |
| 12 kt | 0.16 | -0.028 | 0.26 Nm | 0.08 deg |
| 14.5 kt | 0.11 | -0.023 | 0.31 Nm | 0.09 deg |
| 22.15 kt (top reach) | 0.053 | -0.025 | 0.78 Nm | 0.24 deg |

The design criterion is at most 1 deg at the top-speed case, now the 22 kt top reach at about 0.24 deg (PROPOSED, self-imposed; the flap authority is described in section 3). The twist is nose-down, so it reduces angle of attack (stabilising, not divergent). Flap deflection adds more nose-down moment, not quantified because the solver's flap mode is not enabled. Twist-test the first real arm before building the second (Build Task Tracker).

**What the check changes.** (1) The 537x steel margin and the 0.04 Nm joint moment cover the lift-offset term only. The strut's own side force, which steel.py does not model, adds 0.3 to 2.8 Nm at the joint for 1 to 4 deg of leeway; with it the spigot still clears the 3x bar up to about 3.5 deg of leeway at 14.5 kt and the bond (an assumed strength) up to about 2.5 deg, so the joint looked sound on that basis; under the 38.0 N side force of the 22 kt top reach it is not, so Foilarm_v5 replaces it with the straight-steel layout (the joint item below; section 10). The boss and bore do not weaken the spigot in bending: the bore sits on the neutral axis and the boss adds section. (2) The all-moving foil moves the roll load from a continuous strut-to-foil joint onto a 1.2 mm tang. It yields at 0.99 Nm, a 27 mm shift of the lift's centre of pressure, and meets the 3x bar only up to 9 mm; losing lift over the outer quarter of one half-span (tip ventilation) leaves 1.3x. The 0.3 mm gap between foil and strut does not help (about 9 deg of roll closes it, the tang yields at about 1.5 deg), and neither does the pin, whose axis is along the span. The pivot as modeled does not meet the bar. (3) The pin must be hardened steel with a yield of at least 860 MPa; an A2 or A4 stainless pin is not acceptable. (4) The wire lug is at 2.9x: it is the thinnest feature carrying the full wire load every cycle, so it needs a fatigue look.

**Open decisions from the check.** Fix the roll path or accept the ventilation risk, and choose the pin and wire materials. Thickening the tang alone does not fix it: a 1.8 mm tang in a 2.0 mm slot leaves 0.98 mm walls that limit at about 0.7 to 1.0 Nm. Options to evaluate: a local thicker strut foot (the old root flare gave one), a second tang, a roll stop. MODELLED FIX (Claude, 20 Oct 2026; CAD copy Foilarm_v4b_roll, unsaved, not yet adopted): variant D = 1.6 mm tang in a 1.8 mm slot, tang neck widened (section 10.7 mm2, section modulus 2.95 mm3 against 1.00), and a faired strut-foot pad (rounded rectangle 5.4 mm wide, underside at z 2.85 mm, above the 2.79 mm swept clearance, tapering 8 degrees into the strut by about z 16.6 mm) that gives 1.8 mm slot walls over y 4.8 to 10.8 mm. Hand-calculated roll capacity at 1000 MPa yield: tang 2.9 Nm, walls 2.5 to 3.5 Nm, so 3.3 to 3.8x on the tip-ventilation case and 1.4 to 1.7x on losing a whole half-span. At 750 MPa: 1.9 to 2.2 Nm, 2.4 to 2.8x, which misses the 3x bar. No interference over plus or minus 6 degrees of foil travel. Not FEA; the load cases are assumptions; pin-bore stress concentration and the pad's drag penalty are unchecked. Foilarm_v3, whose steel unit is one solid piece, does not have this weak point.

## Steel-to-carbon joint at the top-reach side force - straight steel in a tapered UD sleeve (layout DECIDED 29 Oct 2026; numbers PROPOSED, coupon test OPEN)

Foilarm_v5 (CAD version 10, 29 Oct 2026; joint_straight.py) has the builder's layout. The old joint (the Foilarm_v4 spigot in a 0.93 mm sleeve that matched the 4 mm wet section) failed on the 22 kt top reach: steel tab 2.2x, sleeve 3.2x, bond 1.6x and the woven carbon just above the end of the steel 1.6x under the 38.0 N strut side force, which puts 3.33 Nm (x1.3) on the joint at 135 mm (strength_v4.py; topreach_checks.py). Now the steel strut keeps its full 22 mm x 18% section straight up to 135 mm, and the carbon is a sleeve on its outside, from a 0.1 mm feather edge at 95 mm to 1.5 mm a side at 135 mm, where the steel ends flat, closing in a short fair wedge (C1, 4.7 x the local thickness long) behind the steel's trailing edge; at 135 mm the strut is 30.8 x 7.0 mm (22.6%) instead of 22 x 4.0 mm. The arm loft starts from that section (hollow from 135 mm, around the old cavity outline) and rejoins the 40 mm/20% sweep about 40 mm along the bend; the CAD loft bulges about 0.3 mm at the leading edge between 40 and 60 mm along the bend, which is cosmetic. Laminate: UD carbon along the strut, about 1.3 mm a side at 135 mm, plies dropped in steps from 135 mm down to 95 mm and run on about 20 mm past the steel's end into the arm before they stop, with one +-45 ply (about 0.2 mm) outside; the glass isolation ply goes on the steel, inside the bond line. One M1.6 x 5 countersunk titanium screw from the side at 125 mm, on the thickest chord station: the head sits in the 1.15 mm of carbon there and the thread in a hole tapped through the steel; it locates the parts and backs up the glue against peel near the steel's end, and the glue carries the load (lower down, the sleeve is too thin to hold a head). Margins on the top-reach side force: glue-line peak 4.2 MPa at the steel's end, 3.6x on the assumed 15 MPa wet strength (bar 3x; shear-lag model, glue G 1000 MPa over 0.2 mm including the glass ply, ASSUMED); the steel inside the sleeve 7.1x on yield even carrying the whole moment alone (bar 3x), about 4x at the screw hole with Kt 2; the all-carbon section just above the steel's end 14.7x in the UD laminate, and the CAD sections along the bend 5.9x or more (lowest 15-25 mm into the bend) even on the woven ply rule (bar 4x), so beyond the UD run-out the normal arm layup is enough. UD is needed for the glue line, not for strength: the same 1.5 mm sleeve in woven ply-rule carbon puts the glue at 1.3x (fails), because a soft sleeve picks up the load in the last few millimetres before the steel's end; all-UD 1.25 mm is at 3.2x. No interference in CAD between the carbon, the steel, the screw and the fork. Cost: +11.3 g per arm (steel +8.4 g for the full section from 105 to 135 mm, carbon +2.9 g, screw 0.05 g), taken from the lead (Weight Budget; it left the all-up CG where it was, then x 491 mm); the thicker wet top of the strut adds 4.9% to the arm drag at 10 kt (0.785 to 0.823 N; 5-7% from 6.5 to 22 kt) and costs a little speed (current figures in 11-performance.md, from joint_straight.py). The scarf proposed first (the steel thinning to an edge under a skin that keeps the outside profile; joint_scarf.py) had the glue at 3.7x on the same shear-lag model (joint_shearlag.py) and was 1.7 g lighter per arm with no drag cost, but needs a 3D-machined taper on the steel and leaves a thin stainless edge; the builder chose the straight steel. Open: peel at the steel's end is not modelled (a 0.5 mm radius on the steel's top edges helps); the glue strength, the UD allowable (at least about 560 MPa wet-laid) and the bond in salt water need the coupon test before the pair is built (section 10); the glue line stays under water at the design ride (isolation ply, sealed feather edge). The foil's pitching moment bends the vertical joint zone about its strong (chordwise) axis, where UD is stiff, so the UD's low shear stiffness adds no measurable twist (under 0.01 deg for the run into the bend, hand estimate).

## Arm ply orientation - OPEN

Recommended for the carbon upper arm: 1 x 0/90 + 5 x bias-cut +-45. The old 3-ply result (0/90 versus bias) no longer applies to this arm: the whole arm twists only about 0.24 deg at the 22 kt top-reach pitching moment (0.09 deg at 14.5 kt), so orientation is not critical for twist. The root doubler (section 3) is a second 0/90 ply, because the root needs bending strength, not torsional stiffness. The joint sleeve (95 mm to about 20 mm past 135 mm) is the exception: UD along the strut with one +-45 ply outside (joint item above), because its stiffness sets the glue-line stress.

## Cant actuator sizing - PROPOSED (28 Oct 2026)

The Actuonix L12 cannot reach the design torque, about 3.8 times the old 3.52 Nm. It is replaced by a self-locking worm drive per arm (Mechanisms - Foil Arm section 1), sized against **13.48 Nm**: 9.9 deg/s raising at an assumed 2 Nm light load, 5.0 deg/s trimming against the static 10.37 Nm and 3.1 deg/s at the design torque, and it holds the load with the motor off. Rev E3 drive (no belt): a Pololu 20D 6 V 63:1 gearmotor through a 2.5:1 spur pair (24T/60T at the rev E2 centres), so its gearbox sees at most 2.9 kg.cm, inside the 3.5 kg.cm continuous rating; the rev E2 2:1 pair would have put it at 3.6 kg.cm at the design torque. The HPC wheel's static rating and face width are still to confirm with HPC: in the upset survival case the wheel has to carry 31.8 Nm (21.2 Nm x 1.5) without tooth yield, which it does with two teeth sharing the load (2.4x) but not with one tooth alone (1.2x). The static top-reach load, 10.37 Nm, is also just above HPC's 10.06 Nm continuous rating at 1000 rpm input, which is for running, not holding. Weight Budget: 196.5 g per arm.

## Rake servo at the top reach - AGF RC A66BHLW, PROPOSED (29 Oct 2026)

The whole-arm rake (Foilarm_v5) has to hold the foil and arm drag 270 mm and 202.5 mm below the rake pin, the E226 pitching moment and the lift offset. On the 22 kt top reach, at the trim rake of -2.95 deg, that is 3.51 Nm steady (foil drag 1.40, arm drag 0.51, pitching moment 0.78, lift offset 0.79 Nm, and about 0.03 Nm for the straight-steel joint's thicker strut top) and 4.56 Nm with x1.3; the rev E2 design case was 2.81 Nm. The Python 125Mini HV (9.0 kg.cm at 8.4 V) could not hold it (79% of its stall torque continuously, 0.98x on the design moment). The rake servo is now the AGF RC A66BHLW (the builder's choice from Claude's comparison, 29 Oct 2026): brushless, IP67, CNC aluminium case, 40.8 x 20.2 x 29.5 mm, 66 g, 25T spline; 25.0 / 30.0 / 33.0 kg.cm and 0.090 / 0.080 / 0.068 s per 60 deg at 6.0 / 7.4 / 8.4 V. Those figures come from retailer listings (no AGF datasheet found), and no current figures are listed. On the same 5:1 linkage (24 mm fork horn, 4.8 mm 7075 servo horn, 10.25 mm link) it has to give 7.2 kg.cm continuously and 9.3 kg.cm at the design moment: the design moment is 3.23x at 7.4 V (2.69x at 6.0 V) and the steady hold is 24% of stall, inside the ASSUMED duty rule (steady hold at or under a third of stall, which needs 21.5 kg.cm at 5:1; the A66BHLW gives 1.40x of that at 7.4 V). Bench-test one with a continuous 7.2 kg.cm hold at 7.4 V, logging case temperature and current, before buying the second (Build Task Tracker). Loaded rake rate on the top reach about 110 deg/s at 7.4 V (145 deg/s unloaded; straight-line torque-speed curve ASSUMED). Linkage at the design moment (190 N in the link, gimbal_revE_calcs.py): the link is 1 mm 301 half-hard stainless, 9.0x in pin bearing (plain 250 MPa steel would be 2.96x, under the 3x bar); the 1.5 mm pins are 3.2x in single shear; the 7075 horn is 8.9x or more. Why this servo: a standard-size servo hits the well wall at 20-45 deg of cant and breaks through the hull bottom from 60 deg unless the wall moves about 10 mm inboard and the cant range is cut to about 55 deg; a 6:1 linkage leaves the servo horn too thin around a 25T spline; the Hitec DB778WP fits the 26.5 mm low-profile envelope but has only 18.0 kg.cm at 7.4 V (40% of stall on the top reach, outside the rule) for $169.99, and the AGF A50BHLW (EUR 69.90) has the same torque; the Chaservo HV3512 (35 kg.cm) is only IP65, and the well floods. Cost: EUR 109.90 per servo; +41.2 g per arm over the Python layout (servo group +42.6 g, cradle -1.4 g), taken from the lead at the same station (the ballast is in section 9); the all-up CG rises 2.3 mm. The case is 3 mm taller than a low-profile servo and the extra height sits inboard, so it sweeps closer to the hull bottom when the arm is raised: in Hull_v2 it breaks through at 70 deg, so the mechanical cant range is now -5 to 67 deg and the PROPOSED software range -2 to 64 deg (2 mm or more inside the skin; item below). No interference with the gimbal over the whole horn throw and cant range (Mechanisms - Foil Arm section 2d). Check on the real servo before the cradle is printed: ear length and hole spacing (CAD assumes 54 mm ears with holes 49 x 10 mm), the spline position (10.25 mm from the case end, 4.5 mm boss) and that the extra height is in the case below the ears, as modelled. The horn needs a pinch-clamp hub, because a centre screw head would hit the link at full throw.

## Cant command path

Settled by the builder (20 Sep 2026): the angle signal is heel (IMU roll), and raise/lower share one channel as a 3-position switch - port arm up / both arms down / starboard arm up. The flight controller reads that channel and drives both actuators (receiver -> flight controller -> actuators) and handles the tack transition. Heel is best run as a slow trim loop (the worm drive trims at about 5.0 deg/s against the static load, item above), with fast ride-height/pitch work staying on the foil angle (the rake servo on Foilarm_v5, the flaps on Foilarm_v3). **Raise direction settled: outboard and up (section 3). Mechanical cant range -5 to 67 deg (PROPOSED; CAD motion study of the gimbal rev E on Hull_v2, 28 Oct 2026, cut from 70 deg on 29 Oct 2026 because the A66BHLW rake servo's taller case breaks through the hull bottom at 70 deg). Software range PROPOSED -2 to 64 deg (29 Oct 2026): that keeps the servo and its cradle 2 mm or more inside the 1-ply skin, against 0.9 mm (cradle, deck) at -5 deg and 0.8 mm (servo, bottom) at 67 deg (Hull_v2 sweep). Still open: where the raised arm stops - PROPOSED 45-64 deg past the down position;** the stop has to stay short of about 90 deg or the raised arm starts costing righting moment. The worm sector (104 deg) covers the whole mechanical range; set the software range conservatively and widen it during tuning.

## Longitudinal layout (CG position) - PROPOSED (28 Oct 2026; re-run 30 Oct 2026 for rudder rev B)

cg_layout.py in the Simulation & Analysis folder; since 30 Oct 2026 it takes its masses and positions from a snapshot of the Weight Budget, columns H-I, so the two give the same CG. Masses and centres of the arms, gimbals, rudder and hull skin come from CAD; the rig and fittings are placed by assumption (mast at 600 mm from the transom); the lead, batteries and electronics are the movable mass. With the lead (about 130 g) inboard of the wet-well walls between the pivot bulkheads, the flight-control bay and 2S battery at about 480-500 mm and the radio bay ahead of the mast, the all-up CG is at x 481.8 mm, z 4.6 mm (about 473-490 mm for a mast at 500-700 mm). That is 44.7 mm aft of the main foil's quarter chord (526.4 mm) and 57.7 mm ahead of the estimated pitch neutral point (424 mm: main foil plus T-foil, no downwash), a static margin of 11.4% of the foil-to-rudder distance. On the 28 Oct layout it was 490.7 mm and 13.2%; the drawn rudder (rev B, 166.5 g at x 43.5, against about 111 g estimated) moved it 9.3 mm aft. The socketed tripod of Hull_v2 version 22 (30 Oct 2026, +1.7 g at the stern, lead 128.4 g) moves it another 0.3 mm, to 481.4 mm; that is inside this model's accuracy, so the figures in this section and section 11 are not re-run for it. At rest the boat trims about 0.44 deg bow down. While foiling, the weight alone would need 2.4 N of T-foil lift, but the sail drive acting high and the drag acting low pitch the bow down (estimated with the 0.395 m centre of effort and a drag centre 200 mm below the waterline, both assumptions), and so does the main foil's own section moment (E226 CM about -0.03 to -0.05 from NeuralFoil). So the T-foil carries a downforce of about 0.7 N at 6.5 kt, 2.6 N at 10 kt and 5.7 N at 14.5 kt with the VPP's own drag at each speed, not the 8.74 N of the 175/550 rule. Over the range of the two assumed heights it is 1.8-3.4 N at 10 kt, a wider band than the 0.5 N the CG move took off. Consequences: up to 14.5 kt the leeward foil carries 28.2-33.2 N and the righting moment is 10.3-12.1 Nm; the downforce keeps growing with speed and passes the old 8.74 N at about 17.7 kt; on the 12 kt top reach (21.9 kt) it is 13.6 N with 41.1 N on the foil, inside the structural basis, which stays at the 29 Oct 2026 values (14.01 N, 41.48 N, section 3). The T-foil works at a lift coefficient of 0.06 or less at every speed (about 0.02 at takeoff), so the old takeoff stall concern is gone. Decision (PROPOSED, 30 Oct 2026): keep the lead at the pivot bulkheads and accept the 482 mm CG for now. It still gives an 11% static margin and a T-foil downforce at every foiling speed, and against the 28 Oct layout it costs 0.1 kt upwind; most of the 30 Oct speed loss, 0.2 kt, comes from the longer wetted rudder blade, not the CG. Putting the CG back at 491 mm with the lead alone would need the lead at x 724, ahead of the mast, and the lead now moves the CG only 4.6 mm per 100 mm. The cheap levers, for when the rig is drawn: the mast step moves the CG 8.8 mm per 100 mm (a mast at 700 mm alone restores 490.5 mm), and the 2S battery in the radio bay at 650 mm adds 4.2 mm.

## Hull half-beam at the arm pivot and a real ballast layout

Half-beam measured on Hull_v2 (28 Oct 2026); ballast layout not yet checked. The lead is now about 128 g, two 64 g blocks of about a 1.8 cm cube each (844 g in two 422 g blocks until 28 Oct 2026), so packaging is fine; the CG layout (item above) puts them inboard of the wet-well walls between the pivot bulkheads.
